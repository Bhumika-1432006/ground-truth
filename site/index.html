    02 · How it works

    Three questions,every monitor, every hour.

    01 · Physics

    Can this reading be real?

    Fine dust (PM2.5) is part of all dust (PM10), so it can never be larger. We also catch values out of range and sensors stuck on one number for hours.

    02 · Neighbours

    Does it agree with the stations around it?

    Hour by hour, against the four nearest stations within 12 km. A station whose daytime readings fall away from its neighbours, more than daytime mixing explains, stands out.

    03 · History

    Has it suddenly changed?

    The last 7 days against the 3 weeks before, for dust and humidity. A sudden shift against its own past is worth a look.

    What a flag means, and what it doesn't. A flag means a station's numbers don't add up against physics, its neighbours or its own past. It does not mean anyone tampered with it: sensors drift, break and get moved. Daytime air mixing makes polluted spots look cleaner by day, so we correct for it. Data comes from the CPCB and DPCC monitors via OpenAQ, refreshed every hour.

    05 · FAQ

    Questions,answered plainly.

    Which number should I use if my station is flagged?

    Use what the four nearest stations read right now. The station panel shows it next to your station's own reading. It is a median of real monitors, not an official reading.

    Does a flag mean someone sprayed water near the monitor?

    No. We tested whether the stations named in the news in October 2025 show a spraying pattern, and with our method they don't stand out. A flag only says the numbers don't add up.

    How fresh is the data?

    The check runs every hour on new readings from the CPCB and DPCC monitors, via OpenAQ. The time of the latest reading is at the top of the page.

    How do you know the checks work?

    We planted a 40% daytime drop in real data at each quiet station, one at a time. It was flagged 30 times out of 30, and newly flagged 3 other stations across all 30 runs. The tests run on every change to the code.

    Is the code open?

    Yes. Everything, including the data analysis behind these checks, is on GitHub.
