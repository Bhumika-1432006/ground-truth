    How it works

    Three questions, asked of every monitor, every hour.

    01 · Physics

    Can this reading be real?

    Fine dust (PM2.5) is part of all dust (PM10), so it can never be larger. We also catch values out of range and sensors stuck on the same number for hours.

    02 · Neighbours

    Does it agree with the stations around it?

    We compare it, hour by hour, with the four nearest stations within 12 km. A station whose daytime readings fall away from its neighbours, more than daytime air mixing explains, stands out.

    03 · History

    Has it suddenly changed?

    We compare the last 7 days with the 3 weeks before, for dust and humidity. A sudden shift against its own past is worth a look.

    What a flag means, and what it doesn't. A flag means a station's numbers don't add up against physics, its neighbours or its own past. It does not mean anyone tampered with it: sensors drift, break and get moved. Daytime air mixing also makes polluted spots look cleaner by day, so we correct for it. Data comes from the CPCB and DPCC monitors via OpenAQ, refreshed every hour; the time of the latest reading is at the top of the page.
    Which number should I use if my station is flagged?

    Use what the four nearest stations read right now. The station panel shows it next to your station's own reading. It is a median of real monitors, not an official reading.

    Does a flag mean someone sprayed water near the monitor?

    No. We tested whether the stations named in the news in October 2025 show a spraying pattern, and with our method they don't stand out. A flag only says the numbers don't add up.

    How do you know the checks work?

    We planted a 40% daytime drop in real data at each quiet station, one at a time. It was flagged 30 times out of 30, and it newly flagged 3 other stations across all 30 runs. The tests run on every change to the code.

    Where does it run?

    On AWS: EventBridge runs a Lambda function every hour that reads new data from OpenAQ, runs the checks and writes the results to S3, served through CloudFront.
